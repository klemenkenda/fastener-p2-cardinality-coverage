# Review: Check for Machine-Generated Text

**Models:** Qwen2.5-0.5B (observer) and Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct (performer), run locally  
**Method:** Binoculars-style score (Hans et al., 2024)  
**Date:** 3 November 2026

**Basis of review.** The check covers the LaTeX sources in [manuscript/sections/](../manuscript/sections/) at commit `d2dea9a`. Sections 00–07 and 09 are identical to that commit. The acknowledgment was scored before the sentence on internal review was added in the working tree. No text was sent to an external service; both models ran on the local CPU. The manuscript was not edited during this review.

## Summary

The detector marks **none of the manuscript** as machine-generated. This result carries no information. Two sets of control text show that the detector fails on this material. First, the abstract, most of the introduction, the discussion, and the conclusion were drafted by an AI assistant in the session before this review, and they are not flagged. Second, [REVIEW-gpt-6.1-sol-pro.md](REVIEW-gpt-6.1-sol-pro.md), which is entirely machine-generated, receives the most human-like scores of all the text tested. The detector therefore cannot say how much of the paper was generated by a language model.

## Verdict

**Inconclusive.** The share of flagged text is 0%, but it would also be 0% for a fully machine-generated paper. The history of the manuscript answers the question better than this detector. It is summarized under "Provenance".

## Method

For each paragraph, the score is the log-perplexity of the text under the performer model, divided by the cross-perplexity between the observer and the performer. Perplexity measures how surprising the text is to a model. The cross-perplexity measures how surprising one model's predictions are to the other. Dividing by it corrects for topics that are hard to predict for any writer. Lower scores mean more machine-like text.

- **Text units.** LaTeX was converted to plain text. Comments, tables, figures, equations, citations, and labels were removed, and inline mathematics was replaced by a placeholder. Paragraphs with fewer than 40 words were skipped. This leaves 81 paragraphs with 9,675 words.
- **Human reference.** Passages of 120 words from the body of Koprivec et al. (2020), the FASTENER paper by the same group, were extracted from the [PDF](../related-work/pdfs/koprivec2020-fastener.pdf). It was written before large language models were in general use. This gives 80 passages with 9,600 words.
- **Machine-generated control.** The first 18 prose paragraphs of [REVIEW-gpt-6.1-sol-pro.md](REVIEW-gpt-6.1-sol-pro.md) contain 1,082 words. Tables, headings, lists, and displayed equations were removed.
- **Threshold.** The detector has no published threshold for these small models. A paragraph counts as flagged if it scores below the 5th percentile of the human reference (0.893). At this threshold, 5% of the human reference is flagged by construction.

The manuscript scores range from 0.931 to 1.306. The lowest manuscript score is still above the threshold. Even at the median of the human reference (1.009), only 4 of the 81 paragraphs would be flagged.

### The controls fail

The ordering of the three groups is the reverse of what a working detector would give:

| Comparison | Probability that a random paragraph of the first group scores lower |
|---|---:|
| Human reference against the manuscript | 0.91 |
| Human reference against the machine-generated control | 0.99 |

The known machine-generated text looks the least machine-like of all. The sections drafted by the assistant (abstract 1.296, discussion 1.132, conclusion 1.132) score above the human reference as well. Two explanations are plausible, and this check cannot separate them. First, models with 0.5 billion parameters are too small for the method; the published thresholds were set for models with 7 billion. Second, the reference differs from the manuscript in more than authorship: it is older, comes from another journal, and was extracted from a PDF with page headers and broken lines, while the manuscript text comes from LaTeX sources.

## Provenance

The history of the repository gives a more reliable account:

- **Commits.** All 10 commits that change the manuscript carry an AI co-author trailer.
- **Drafted by an AI assistant in the previous session:** the abstract, the discussion, the conclusion, and the introduction except its contribution list and scope paragraph.
- **Commit message.** Commit `d2dea9a` states "Abstract, discussion and conclusion written by the author". These three sections were drafted by the assistant.
- **Acknowledgment.** [08-acknowledgments.tex](../manuscript/sections/08-acknowledgments.tex) states AI assistance with programming, experiment tracking, evaluations, draft reports, the review of related work and the organization of the Related Work section, summaries of methods and datasets, visualizations, and internal review. It does not mention drafting the abstract, introduction, discussion, and conclusion.

## Actions to consider

1. Amend the message of commit `d2dea9a` (or note it in the next commit) so that it does not attribute the abstract, discussion, and conclusion to the author alone.
2. Extend the AI-use statement in the acknowledgment to cover help with drafting the abstract, introduction, discussion, and conclusion. Alternatively, rewrite those sections yourself and keep the statement as it is.
3. Do not cite this check as evidence that the paper reads as human-written. A detector whose controls fail gives no evidence either way.
4. If a journal or institution requires a detector result, use the tool it specifies. Check first that its terms allow uploading an unpublished manuscript.

## Reproduction

The script and the scores are outside the repository, in the session folder `files\detector\` (`binoculars.py`, `scores.csv`, `scores_control.csv`), with an isolated Python environment (PyTorch 2.15 CPU, Transformers 5.22). The models are stored in the local Hugging Face cache (about 2 GB). Scoring the manuscript and the reference took about 45 minutes on 12 CPU threads in bfloat16, with paragraphs truncated at 512 tokens.
